Choosing Parts for Your PC

Start With Your Budget

Before choosing parts, you should decide how much money you want to spend on your computer. Your budget will help determine how much you can spend on each component and where you should prioritize performance.

For a gaming PC, the graphics card will usually take up a large part of the budget. You should also make sure that the components you choose are compatible with each other.

Example PC Part Options

The table below gives examples of the types of components you might choose depending on your budget. These are general examples and are meant to show how a build can change as the budget increases.

Component Budget Option Higher-End Option
CPU 6-Core Processor 8-Core Processor
GPU Entry-Level Gaming GPU High-End Gaming GPU
RAM 16 GB 32 GB
Storage 1 TB SSD 2 TB SSD
Power Supply 650 Watt 850 Watt

Check Compatibility

Not every PC component will work with every other component. Your CPU must use the correct motherboard socket, your RAM must be supported by the motherboard, and your case needs enough space for the parts you choose.

Websites such as PCPartPicker can help check compatibility between components before you purchase them.
